Methodology

This document records the mathematical formulation behind the ACVRP-CO2 project. The notation used here matches the variable names in the source code so that readers can move between the paper and the implementation without translation overhead.

Let G=(V,A)G = (V, A)G=(V,A) be a directed graph where V={0,1,…,n}V = \{0, 1, \ldots, n\}V={0,1,…,n} is the set of customers (with index 000 reserved for the depot) and A⊆V×VA \subseteq V \times VA⊆V×V is the set of feasible arcs. A homogeneous fleet of KKK vehicles, each with capacity QQQ, is based at the depot.

For each arc (i,j)∈A(i, j) \in A(i,j)∈A we define a distance dij(X)d_{ij}^{(X)}dij(X)​ according to variant X∈{SE,SM,SR,AR}X \in \{SE, SM, SR, AR\}X∈{SE,SM,SR,AR} defined below.

2. Distance Variants

SE — Symmetric Euclidean. The great-circle distance between two points (φi,λi)(\varphi_i, \lambda_i)(φi​,λi​) and (φj,λj)(\varphi_j, \lambda_j)(φj​,λj​) in spherical coordinates is computed via the Haversine formula:

dijSE=2Rarcsin⁡ ⁣sin⁡2 ⁣φj−φi2+cos⁡φicos⁡φjsin⁡2 ⁣λj−λi2,d_{ij}^{SE} = 2 R \arcsin\!\sqrt{\sin^2\!\frac{\varphi_j - \varphi_i}{2} + \cos\varphi_i \cos\varphi_j \sin^2\!\frac{\lambda_j - \lambda_i}{2}}, dijSE​=2Rarcsinsin22φj​−φi​​+cosφi​cosφj​sin22λj​−λi​​​,

with RRR the mean Earth radius. Symmetric by construction.

SM — Symmetric Manhattan. Project lat/lon onto a local equirectangular plane centred at the mean latitude φˉ\bar\varphiφˉ​, then take the L1L^1L1 norm:

dijSM=∣xi−xj∣+∣yi−yj∣,d_{ij}^{SM} = |x_i - x_j| + |y_i - y_j|, dijSM​=∣xi​−xj​∣+∣yi​−yj​∣,

with the projection scales chosen so that one degree of latitude or longitude corresponds to the appropriate metric distance at φˉ\bar\varphiφˉ​.

AR — Asymmetric Road. Let GR=(VR,AR)G_R = (V_R, A_R)GR​=(VR​,AR​) be the directed road-network graph from OSM, with edge weights given by physical street length. Snap each customer to its nearest node and define

dijAR=ShortestPathGR(ν(i)→ν(j))d_{ij}^{AR} = \text{ShortestPath}_{G_R}(\nu(i) \to \nu(j)) dijAR​=ShortestPathGR​​(ν(i)→ν(j))

where ν:V→VR\nu: V \to V_Rν:V→VR​ is the snap mapping. Because GRG_RGR​ honours one-way streets and turn restrictions, dijAR≠djiARd_{ij}^{AR} \neq d_{ji}^{AR}dijAR​=djiAR​ in general.

SR — Symmetric Road. Average the two directions of AR:

dijSR=12(dijAR+djiAR).d_{ij}^{SR} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(d_{ij}^{AR} + d_{ji}^{AR}\bigr). dijSR​=21​(dijAR​+djiAR​).

3. CVRP Decision Model

Let xijk∈{0,1}x_{ij}^k \in \{0, 1\}xijk​∈{0,1} indicate whether vehicle kkk traverses arc (i,j)(i, j)(i,j). The standard CVRP is the mixed-integer program

min⁡∑k=1K∑(i,j)∈Acij xijk\min \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{(i,j) \in A} c_{ij} \, x_{ij}^k mink=1∑K​(i,j)∈A∑​cij​xijk​

subject to

∑k=1K∑j∈Vxijk=1∀ i∈V∖{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{j \in V} x_{ij}^k = 1 \qquad \forall\, i \in V \setminus \{0\} k=1∑K​j∈V∑​xijk​=1∀i∈V∖{0}

(every customer is visited exactly once),

∑j∈Vxijk−∑j∈Vxjik=0∀ i∈V, k=1,…,K\sum_{j \in V} x_{ij}^k - \sum_{j \in V} x_{ji}^k = 0 \qquad \forall\, i \in V,\ k = 1, \ldots, K j∈V∑​xijk​−j∈V∑​xjik​=0∀i∈V, k=1,…,K

(flow conservation per vehicle),

∑i∈Vqi∑j∈Vxijk≤Q∀ k\sum_{i \in V} q_i \sum_{j \in V} x_{ij}^k \le Q \qquad \forall\, k i∈V∑​qi​j∈V∑​xijk​≤Q∀k

(capacity), plus the customary sub-tour elimination constraints (we omit the formal MTZ statement here; OR-Tools enforces them implicitly via its routing model).

4. Multi-Objective Arc Cost

The arc cost cijc_{ij}cij​ combines a distance term and a fuel term:

cij=α1 dij+α2 Yij,c_{ij} = \alpha_1\, d_{ij} + \alpha_2\, Y_{ij}, cij​=α1​dij​+α2​Yij​,

where α1+α2=1\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = 1α1​+α2​=1 and the fuel burned on an arc is

Yij=C1 dij+C2 tij+C3 Mij dij.Y_{ij} = C_1\, d_{ij} + C_2\, t_{ij} + C_3\, M_{ij}\, d_{ij}. Yij​=C1​dij​+C2​tij​+C3​Mij​dij​.

Here tij=dij/vt_{ij} = d_{ij} / vtij​=dij​/v is the arc-traversal time (with vvv an average urban speed), MijM_{ij}Mij​ is the payload (kg) the vehicle is carrying while on the arc, and C1,C2,C3C_1, C_2, C_3C1​,C2​,C3​ are linear coefficients calibrated to a light-duty diesel van. Total emissions are E=ρ∑YijE = \rho \sum Y_{ij}E=ρ∑Yij​ where ρ\rhoρ is the diesel CO₂ factor (default 2.682.682.68 kg CO₂ per litre).

This linear form is a simplification of the load-aware emissions model of Bektaş & Laporte (2011); it deliberately drops the speed-cubed aerodynamic term so the objective remains linear in dijd_{ij}dij​ and tractable for OR-Tools' arc-cost callback.

5. The Asymmetry Penalty

For each variant XXX we solve the CVRP with arc costs computed from d(X)d^{(X)}d(X), obtaining a route plan RX\mathcal{R}_XRX​. We then re-evaluate RX\mathcal{R}_XRX​ using the ground-truth d(AR)d^{(AR)}d(AR) to obtain the "real" distance DXD_XDX​ and emissions EXE_XEX​. The asymmetry penalty is

ηXD=DX−DARDAR,ηXE=EX−EAREAR.\eta_X^{D} = \frac{D_X - D_{AR}}{D_{AR}}, \qquad \eta_X^{E} = \frac{E_X - E_{AR}}{E_{AR}}. ηXD​=DAR​DX​−DAR​​,ηXE​=EAR​EX​−EAR​​.

These two scalars are the headline numbers of the empirical study.

6. Solvers

Exact / heuristic. OR-Tools' constraint-programming routing model with PATH_CHEAPEST_ARC first-solution heuristic and GUIDED_LOCAL_SEARCH metaheuristic, time-limited to 60 seconds.

Genetic Algorithm. Permutation chromosome decoded via Beasley's split into capacity-feasible routes; ordered crossover (OX1); position-wise swap mutation; tournament selection with elitism.

7. Asymmetry Index

A=∑i<j∣dijAR−djiAR∣∑i<j(dijAR+djiAR),\mathcal{A} = \frac{\sum_{i < j} \bigl|d_{ij}^{AR} - d_{ji}^{AR}\bigr|} {\sum_{i < j} \bigl(d_{ij}^{AR} + d_{ji}^{AR}\bigr)}, A=∑i<j​(dijAR​+djiAR​)∑i<j​​dijAR​−djiAR​​​,

bounded in [0,1][0, 1][0,1]. A value near 000 means the road network is practically symmetric; values closer to 111 indicate one-way streets dominate.
